# MELANCHOLY FALLS
### A Very Serious Drama — Series Bible v1

> *"Every town has secrets. This one has a quarry."*

---

## THE PITCH (say this on the itch page)

**MELANCHOLY FALLS** is a visual novel parody of the late-90s/early-2000s teen drama —
the golden age of the WB: *Smallville*, *Dawson's Creek*, *One Tree Hill*, *The O.C.*,
*Everwood*, *Gilmore Girls* — played with the emotional gravity of a war documentary and
the restraint of *Family Guy*. It is a show about a pretty town, a wet creek, a deep
quarry, and the meteor shower that ruined everyone's hair.

No anime. No dated high-school archetypes played straight. Every trope is loaded,
aimed at its own foot, and fired.

- **Genre:** Comedy / Parody / Interactive Drama
- **Format:** Episodic visual novel, 20–30 min per episode, multiple endings each
- **Platform:** itch.io (browser + downloadable), 1280×720, desktop + mobile
- **Rating:** TV-14 energy. Edgy, dark, stupid. No slurs, no explicit content.

---

## THE TOWN

**Melancholy Falls** — a small American town (population: felt). Corn. A creek where
all important conversations legally must occur. A quarry where drama goes to die.
The state is never named: every time a character is about to say the state, a church
bell rings and we cut away. This is never explained.

**The Drizzle of '89** — sixteen years ago, a meteor shower the town refuses to call
a meteor shower ("shower" felt needy). It took the Luxury family greenhouse, most of
Dex's hair, the town's ability to have a normal Tuesday, and deposited a naked baby
in a cornfield holding an empty briefcase.

Landmarks:
- **The Creek** — town's confessional. Has its own parking.
- **The Quarry** — deep, wet, dramatic. Owned by the Stone family. OSHA-visible.
- **Longing High** — the high school. The wrong side of the tracks runs *through* it; the wrong side is the cafeteria.
- **The Drowsy Otter (Bait & Scone)** — a shop that sells fishing bait and scones, because the two things men refuse to choose between.
- **The Autumn Chills Dance** — annual school dance sponsored by Dex Luxury, who owns the word "chills" in the county.

## THE CAST

### BRAM WELLINGTON (17) — the hero
Found naked in a cornfield as a baby, holding an empty briefcase. Raised by Ma and Pa.
Kind, handsome, flannel-forward, and **cursed with The Feels**: whenever Bram feels an
emotion — any emotion — physics feels it too. One (1) feeling = every loose object within
fifty feet levitates, bends, or explodes. He has never once been calm. Teen hormones are,
therefore, a zoning issue.
*(Secretly: the empty briefcase is the real mystery of the series. Everyone fixates on the meteors. The briefcase is the thing.)*

### TUESDAY WINTERS (17) — the new girl
Arrives in Episode 1 with a binder ("LONGING ANOMALIES — VOL. 1") and a tape recorder.
Keeps a "Boring Facts (For Emergencies)" appendix. Her family moves constantly — toward
weirdness, never away from it — because **Tuesday is impossible to photograph: every
photo ever taken of her is a slightly different goose.** Her power, and her shame.

### DEX LUXURY (24) — the town's bald young billionaire
Lost his hair, his mother's greenhouse, and his ability to shiver in the Drizzle of '89.
He has been "room temperature" for sixteen years. Charitable in a way that constitutes a
crime ("The orphans are still on the yacht. They've formed a government."). Fixates on
Bram instantly, the way a lonely rich man fixates on the one interesting thing in town.
Every conversation with Dex pivots into a shakedown for one of his foundations.

### JUNIE LAKE (17) — the girl next door
Speaks **only in gravelly WB voiceover narration, out loud, in real time.** Cannot turn it
off. ("I walked the long way to school. The creek understood me. — Can you move? You're
in my narration.") Her family runs the Drowsy Otter. Secretly the most reliable person
in town; the narration never lies, it just editorializes.

### CHAD MORGAN (17) — the coma boy
Been in a coma since "the pilot episode of our lives." His hospital bed is parked by the
trophy case and wheeled to fourth period because "attendance is a legal thing." His toes
twitch whenever Bram uses The Feels within forty feet — no one has noticed except the
school nurse. Chad wakes up at the worst possible moment. Chad is, when awake, the most
well-adjusted person in town, which is its own indictment.

### BRICK STONE (18) — the bully
Football captain. Punches lockers "to keep them honest." His father owns the quarry.
His mother is a rumor. Has a good heart, buried under a career of concussions, buried
under a second, larger career of concussions.

### MA (MARJORIE) & PA (GERALD) WELLINGTON — the parents
Ma communicates **exclusively in casseroles.** There is a casserole dictionary. Lasagna:
"your father lost the farm." Tater tot hotdish: "the meteor insurance lapsed." Jell-O
with a fork in it: "someone died." She is fluent; the family is fluent; it is never
remarked upon as strange. Pa's nemesis is the Bank of Melancholy Falls. The Bank won a
staring contest in 1997. The east field has never come home.

### PRINCIPAL SKYLER GRANT (34) — the principal
Has **Cliffhanger's Disease**: can only end sentences as cliffhangers. "Attention
students. This morning... something happened. That is all. For now." Doctors say he'll
be fine. For now.

### NURSE BARGAIN — the school nurse
Keeps a journal. Observes everything. Says almost nothing. ("The boy in bed three is a
mood ring.") She will be the one who figures it all out, in season four, to no fanfare.

## THE COMEDY RULES (how we keep it Family Guy, not Lazy)

1. **Play the drama 100% straight.** The camera never winks. The *content* winks
   constantly. Gravelly narration over a boy buying chips. Slow-motion walks for
   everything: new girl arrival, casserole reveals, a goose being arrested.
2. **Cutaway gags are a gameplay verb.** "This is worse than the time Pa entered a
   staring contest with the Bank." HARD WIPE → 2–4 line mini-scene in crude doodle
   style → wipe back → someone says "Anyway." Cutaways are unlockable in a gallery.
3. **Escalate specificity.** Not "the town is weird" — "the man who is 40% soup
   (hot tub incident; he's fine; he's broth now)."
4. **Every trope gets exactly one loving reenactment before it's detonated.** The
   rain-in-the-slow-dance, the freeze-frame character intro, the "you don't know what
   happened THAT NIGHT" — we do the version the WB would be proud of, then we keep
   pulling the thread past the point of sanity.
5. **THAT NIGHT.** Every character references "that night." No two characters are
   referring to the same night. Series-long runner. In Ep 6 we learn every single
   "that night," and every single one is a Chili's closing.
6. **The state name** can never be spoken (church bell gag, above).
7. **Nobody acknowledges the casseroles as strange. Ever.**
8. **The Drama Meter is diegetic.** When it fills, someone MUST say something
   devastating and leave — even if they have to generate rain indoors. Rain follows
   one character specifically. We will not say who. It's Chad's mom? It's nobody. Moving on.

## EPISODE 1 — "PILOT" (the game you're holding)

**Cold open:** Narrator establishes town, meteor "Drizzle," baby-in-cornfield-with-briefcase,
young Dex losing his hair in tragic slow motion while drinking juice ("It's juice. I'm eight.").

**Act One:** Bram's morning (Casserole Number Six, "We need to talk"); the toaster
ascends when Ma gives him a sweet card; walk to school; Junie narrates at him; Principal
Grant's cliffhanger announcements; Chad's bed by the trophy case; **Tuesday arrives** —
slow-mo, freeze-frame lower-third, a kid whispers "she has city shoes." Bram feels one
(1) feeling; Dex's flagpole bends into a question mark. Dex arrives by helicopter
("I own the school's air rights"). The Autumn Chills Dance is announced. Tuesday's
tape recorder: "Day one. I've found him. Or someone like him."

**Act Two:** The Creek summit with Junie (choice #1 — how to handle Tuesday's questions).
Dex's mansion: fencing a Roomba, the meteor display case labeled "MY HAIR," "forever room
temperature" monologue. The Drowsy Otter: Tuesday's binder of anomalies — the hail cow,
the soup man, the indoor funeral rain (choice #2 — lie / deflect / half-confess).
Ma's Casserole Number Twelve: "Your father wants to talk. At the dance. In front of people."
Chad's toe-twitch subplot; Nurse Bargain's journal.

**Act Three:** The Autumn Chills Dance in the gym (which is built over the quarry).
The banner reads AUTUMN CHIL because the L keeps falling ("It's become a metaphor,
which the decorating committee refuses to address"). Brick punches the punch ("someone
had to"). During the slow dance — **Chad wakes up**, points at Bram: "IT'S HIM. THE
FEELINGS COME FROM HIM." The floor gives way. Chad falls into the quarry. Bram can't
act (feelings too big) — someone reads Boring Facts until Bram goes completely numb and
performs the rescue at deadpan tax-account energy. Then the climax choice, and **four
endings** (see below). Post-credits sting in every ending: Principal Grant, alone, at
home: "Today, a boy became a man. Or did he. ...For now."

### THE FOUR ENDINGS
- **THE PROM KING OF TRUTH** — Bram tells everyone everything on the DJ mic. The town
  shrugs (they've survived worse; the soup man is a *beloved* soup man). Dex slow-claps
  alone, which is how he claps.
- **THE CREEK PACT** — Bram tells only Tuesday, at the creek. Tuesday counters with her
  secret (the photographs are all geese). The two most dangerous freaks in town decide
  to be weird at each other on purpose. Best ending; the "to be continued" means it.
- **THE GOOSE ALIBI** — Bram blames a goose. The town *needs* this. A goose is arrested
  in Bram's place and given its own slow-mo perp walk (freeze-frame: "GOOSE — did it").
  Bram broods; his chair is moved to the wrong side of the cafeteria tracks. "DARK BRAM RISES."
- **THE CASSEROLE** *(secret; unlock by collecting 3+ casserole meanings)* — Bram does
  what Ma would do: presents Chad with Casserole Number Nine, "I have powers, and I love
  you." Chad eats it, nods, and voluntarily goes back into his coma: "I saw everything,
  you know. Everything. Wake me when it's over."

### METERS (visible, diegetic, stupid)
- **DRAMA** — fills when you choose the dramatic option. At overflow: forced devastating
  exit + localized weather.
- **SUSPICION** — how close Tuesday is to confirming Bram. High suspicion unlocks the
  sharper versions of Act Two scenes.
- **BOND** — Tuesday warmth. Gates THE CREEK PACT ending's best flavor.
- **CASSEROLE** (hidden) — number of casserole meanings learned. Gates the secret ending.

## ART DIRECTION (for the professional in the room)

**Placeholders ship in this build** so the game is playable today; every image is a
drop-in replacement — **same filename, same dimensions, done.**

- **Backgrounds:** 1920×1080, painterly, golden-hour WB promo-photography lighting,
  autumnal muted palette, soft grain. Nobody is ever not backlit.
- **Sprites:** ~1200×2000 transparent PNG, full body, western animation lineage
  (thick clean line, flat color, painterly shading) — absolutely not anime. WB wardrobe
  2001–2004: flannel over tees, corduroy, cord necklaces, inconvenient scarves.
- **Cutaway art:** deliberately crude ballpoint doodle style, construction-paper frames.
  The gap between the drama art and the cutaway art IS the joke.
- Titles/logo: WB-grade distressed serif, meteor streak, wet leaves.

## SOUND
Procedural WebAudio (no audio files shipped): the record-scratch cutaway wipe, the
dramatic sting, the church bell, rain noise, and a tasteful sad-piano loop for menus
and creek scenes. All toggleable; all replaceable with real files later by dropping
files into `assets/snd/` (hooks already in the engine).
